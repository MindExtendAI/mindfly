# Walking model

[Documentation index](README.md)

MindFly uses EEG or a generated demo score to stimulate a reduced neural circuit.
The circuit's output drives a fly walking in a circular arena. This guide separates
the neural model, the movement rules, and the experimental sources.

## From focus to stimulation

Both live EEG and demo mode use the same control rules:

| Setting | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Usable focus score of at least 65% |
| Input frequency | 10 Hz: one pulse every 100 ms |
| Pulse width | 5 ms |
| Input amplitude | Assumed model drive of 2 mV/ms |
| Maximum bout | 30 seconds of model time |
| Rearming | Focus must fall below the threshold before another completed bout can start |

Lower focus or unusable/disconnected EEG stops walking. Background-tab suspension
also stops control. The 10 Hz input is generated by the app; it is not the raw EEG
waveform and does not prescribe every neuron's firing rate.

The gate is implemented in [lib/bpn-walking.ts](../lib/bpn-walking.ts).
The neural dynamics run in [Rust/WASM](../rust/neural-engine/README.md).

## Reduced neural circuit

The [circuit dataset](../public/data/bpn-walking.json) contains **54 neurons and
266 directed connections** selected from a full-CNS simulation benchmark.
Connection weights retain recorded synapse counts and predicted neurotransmitter
signs; the electrical response parameters are model assumptions.

Twenty-four cells receive stimulation. They belong to the candidate BPN types
CL208, CL209, SMP459, SMP460 and SMP461. Their correspondence to the genetic driver
in [Bidaye et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2020.07.032) remains unresolved.
The other 30 cells are downstream members of the reduced circuit. No active leg
motor neurons were present in this benchmark.

The model advances in 0.5 ms steps and returns activity in 50 ms bins. A weighted
readout provides a forward command. The source engine revision and graph hash
are retained in the dataset; [ExportBpn.java](../scripts/ExportBpn.java) records
the extraction procedure.

## Body movement and legs

The fly preserves its heading inside the arena and turns near the rim. It has no
goal-seeking behavior in the current walking mode. The arena is a flat circle
44 mm in diameter; it approximates the study's bowl assay without reproducing
its three-dimensional mechanics. The fly is rendered at three times its native mesh scale.

Forward command is mapped to speed using an authored gain of 30. Boundary turning,
speed smoothing and the body trajectory are software rules, not measured physiology.
See [the walking controller](../lib/bpn-walking.ts).

The [gait model](../lib/xenova/walking-kinematics.js) uses a 40 ms swing and a
speed-dependent stance. Opposite-side legs alternate, and inverse kinematics
positions the articulated limbs. Gait assumptions are informed by
[DeAngelis et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.46409), but are not fitted to
that dataset. Leg stepping is independent of the 10 Hz stimulation frequency.

Wings stay folded. Muscles, ground-contact forces, proprioception and a complete
motor circuit are not simulated. Walking duration, speed distributions and
individual variability have not been fitted to the Bidaye experiment.

## What the checks establish

[Neural reference tests](../tests/bpn.test.mjs) compare the reduced Rust circuit
with independent full-CNS Java outputs, including a 45-second stimulation benchmark
and a 23-second interrupted-input run. Removing BPN output connections eliminates
downstream spikes and forward output in that model.

Other checks cover stopping, bounded planar movement, coordinate registration,
WASM failures and [leg kinematics](../tests/walking-kinematics.test.mjs).
Run them with `npm test` from the repository root.

These checks establish software consistency. They do not validate the candidate
BPN mapping, biological spike timing, attention measurement or real-fly behavior.
